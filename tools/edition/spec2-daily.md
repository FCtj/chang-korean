# Daily-life course: same task as spec2.md, with these differences

Read `spec2.md` in this folder first and follow all of it. The chunk names here are `end-N` (English) and `jad-N` (Japanese).

## English daily course (`end-*`)

- **Source.** Words come from the CEFR-J Vocabulary Profile:
  - A1 is beginner / middle-school year 1.
  - A2 is middle school.
  - B1 is high school.
- **`hint`.** Shows the CEFR level, part of speech, and sometimes a theme. Give the **most common everyday sense** for a Korean middle- or high-school student, not a business sense. If the hint lists two parts of speech, you may give both, e.g. `[동] 놀다 · [명] 연극`.
- **Examples.** Simple, natural sentences about everyday or school life:
  - home, friends, school, hobbies, shopping, travel, feelings;
  - A1/A2 sentences use easy grammar;
  - 12 words or fewer is preferred.
- **`taken`.** Each unit has `taken.emoji` and `taken.meaning`. Other words in the same unit already use these, so **do not reuse them**.

## Japanese daily course (`jad-*`)

- **Source.** Words are common everyday Japanese (JMdict common-word marks, ordered by Jiten frequency) that are **not** on the JLPT N5–N1 lists. Many are conversational or modern words.
- **`hint`.** Follow the hint sense. When a word is mostly spoken or casual (そっか, えっと, すっごい), say so briefly in the meaning, e.g. `(말) 그렇구나`.
- **Examples.** Natural everyday sentences, 30 characters or fewer. Casual speech is fine when the word is casual, and the Korean translation should match that register.

Validate with `check-chunk.mjs <name>` exactly as in spec2.md.
